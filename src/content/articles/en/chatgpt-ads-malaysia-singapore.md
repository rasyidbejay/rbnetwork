ChatGPT Ads began rolling out in Malaysia and Singapore on 23 September. OpenAI lists self-service Ads Manager as available in both countries, the minimum daily budget is MYR 35 or SGD 25, and ads show only to people on the Free and Go plans. What is missing is just as useful to know: OpenAI publishes no price per click and no performance benchmarks yet.

This guide sticks to what OpenAI has said and what its help centre shows. Where we are reading between the lines, we say so.

## What launched, and where

On 23 September OpenAI said ChatGPT Ads would begin rolling out in Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam and Taiwan, bringing availability to more than 60 countries. On 21 October it expanded again to 26 more countries, making 89, and said over 100,000 advertisers had run campaigns since launch, just over eight months earlier.

The product is young. OpenAI's help centre calls Ads Manager a beta and describes a focused pilot from February through April before self-serve onboarding began.

## Who sees the ads

Only people on the Free and Go plans. OpenAI says Plus, Pro and Enterprise subscriptions remain ad-free. It also says ads are clearly labelled and separate from ChatGPT's answers, that people can control ad personalisation, and that ad-free paid plans exist. ChatGPT has 1.2 billion weekly users worldwide, according to OpenAI, but in the posts we read it gives no user numbers for Malaysia or Singapore, so local reach is unknown.

## How to buy

The 23 September post names three routes: OpenAI's Ads Solutions team, agency partners including dentsu, Havas Media, Omnicom Media, Publicis Groupe and WPP, and technology partners. Self-service Ads Manager is open to eligible businesses.

For eligibility, OpenAI's availability page is the clearest source. It lists both Malaysia and Singapore as available for self-service on 26 October, and it adds a condition: the legal entity that will advertise and be billed must be based in a listed country. A separate help page says some new accounts can at first advertise only in their home country, and unlock others after identity verification and a spend threshold that it does not specify.

The sign-up page describes three steps: create a campaign with a budget and goal, add your ad details by bulk upload or in the tool, then launch and learn. Ads must pass review before they serve, and an unverified account or a billing problem can also stop them.

## Formats, measurement and cost

You choose one of three objectives. Views is billed per 1,000 impressions, Clicks per valid click, and Conversions per valid click or per 1,000 impressions while optimising towards a chosen event. You never pay per conversion. Budgets can be a campaign total or a daily figure, and a daily budget is an average over seven days, with spend capped at twice the daily figure in a day and seven times in a week.

The minimum daily budget depends on your billing currency. OpenAI's campaign page lists MYR 35 and SGD 25. We did not find a published price per click or per thousand impressions, and OpenAI's own FAQ says there are no performance benchmarks across advertisers or industries yet. Our reading: anyone quoting an average cost for ChatGPT Ads in Malaysia is guessing or relying on a single account.

On 5 October OpenAI announced a visual ad format, shown during image generation to Free and Go users. It said testing would begin later in October in the US with an initial group of advertisers, so it is not yet a Malaysian or Singaporean option, in our reading.

The same post expanded measurement. Integrations with Hightouch, Tealium and LiveRamp help advertisers send conversion data, and attribution partners include AppsFlyer, Triple Whale and Adjust, among others. OpenAI also cites three partner-reported results, each for a single advertiser. One, from DV Rockerbox, said WeightWatchers' attributed cost per acquisition was 15.3% lower than its blended paid-search benchmark. Treat these as vendor-reported case studies, not benchmarks.

## What OpenAI says about privacy and answers

OpenAI says it keeps conversations private from advertisers, does not sell customer data, and does not let advertising influence ChatGPT's answers. It says placement guardrails keep ads out of emotionally vulnerable or sensitive contexts, and that Negative Phrases are available to qualifying advertisers. It is also developing brand suitability pilots with DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science. These are OpenAI's statements. We have not seen an independent audit of them.

## Paid ads or earning citations?

The two are not substitutes. Because OpenAI says ads do not influence answers, paying cannot buy a place in what ChatGPT writes. A paid ad is a labelled placement. A citation is earned, and it depends on your pages. That is why we read this as a choice about timing and measurement, not a contest.

In plain terms: if you need results within weeks and your analytics can record what an ad visitor does, a small test with a fixed budget and a stop date is reasonable. If you cannot measure sales or leads, you would be paying blind, so fix tracking first. If you have more time, invest in pages worth citing, as we explain in [GEO vs SEO in 2026](/ai-search/geo-vs-seo-2026/) and [SEO, AEO and GEO explained](/seo/seo-aeo-geo-explained/). Doing both is allowed.

## What we still do not know

We do not know the price per click or per thousand impressions in MYR or SGD. We do not know how quickly a small business is approved, or the spend threshold that unlocks advertising outside a home country. We found no statement on whether ads can be written in Malay. We do not know when the visual format will reach Malaysia and Singapore, or how many Malaysians and Singaporeans use the Free and Go plans. OpenAI's sign-up page showed a new-advertiser ad credit when we visited, in a currency for another country, so we cannot say whether an equivalent offer exists here. We will update this piece as better evidence appears.
