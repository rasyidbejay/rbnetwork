On 15 October Google announced five changes to AI Mode and AI Overviews that are meant to send more people on to websites. Google presents them as changes to how links and sources are shown, and it gives no click figures. What you can do is check your Search Console setting, make your pages worth the link and know which changes are still only tests.

This is a news piece, so we stick to what Google has said and mark the places where we are reading between the lines.

## What changed on 15 October

Hema Budaraju, a vice president of product management at Google, published the update on Google's blog under the title "More ways to connect with the web from Search". It says AI Mode and AI Overviews are getting clearer inline links, more context about sources and more timely articles. It also says the number of people using the Preferred Sources feature has grown by more than seven times since the start of the year.

## The five changes, one by one

1. **Inline links, shown higher.** Inline links sit inside the words of an AI answer. Google says it has made substantial increases to how often they appear, and it is now launching updates that show them higher up in a response more frequently.
2. **A section for official pages.** Google will test a dedicated section at the top of an answer for the one or two official places to complete a task, with a note on why each link is useful. It gives no timing.
3. **Carousels of timely articles.** The prominent carousels for timely articles are starting to roll out to more locations and languages globally. They also highlight a reader's Preferred Sources. Google names no countries.
4. **Related articles, made more prominent.** The links at the end of some AI responses get image thumbnails and a more clickable design.
5. **Creator and publisher names.** Google started testing clickable names on video results in AI Mode that link to Search profiles. Eligible creators and publishers can read about Search profiles on Google's documentation site.

## Which changes are live and which are tests

Google uses different verbs for each change, and the difference matters if you plan around them.

| Change | What Google says | Scope Google gives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inline links shown higher | Launching updates | Not stated |
| Section for official pages | Will be tested | Not stated |
| Carousels of timely articles | Starting to roll out | Additional locations and languages globally, none named |
| Related-article links | Being made more prominent | Not stated |
| Creator and publisher names | Testing started that week | AI Mode video results, locations not stated |

Our reading: only two are explicitly tests, but "rolling out" does not mean every reader sees a change today. We could not confirm from the post which countries get what, or when.

## Preferred Sources: more than a news feature

Preferred Sources lets people choose the sites they want to see more often. When 9to5Google reported its global expansion on 30 April, Google said readers were twice as likely to click through to a site after marking it as preferred, and that over 200,000 sites had been selected. We have not seen the method behind the "twice as likely" figure, so treat it as Google's claim.

What is new is the reach. Google's documentation says a preferred site can be highlighted in Top Stories, and in AI Mode and AI Overviews for the people who selected it. The October post adds that the carousels for timely articles highlight a reader's Preferred Sources too.

Two conditions in the documentation are easy to miss. Your site must be included in Search generative AI features in Search Console to be eligible. And only a whole domain or a subdomain qualifies, not a folder such as `/blog`.

We also checked. On 26 October we searched Google's source preferences tool for rbnetwork.my and it returned no results, while Search Engine Journal appeared. The tool says sources that are not updated regularly may be unavailable. We do not know why our domain is missing, or what decides which sites appear. Until it shows up we will not ask readers to add us.

## What this means for clicks

Google's post makes no claim about traffic. Independent studies before this update found fewer clicks per search when an AI summary appears, as we set out in [GEO vs SEO in 2026](/ai-search/geo-vs-seo-2026/). Both things can be true: a link that is easier to see inside an answer that gets fewer clicks overall.

Reporting is also limited. A report from Search Engine Journal on 17 October notes that Search Console gives each link in an AI Overview the position of the whole overview, so a link moved higher will not look better in the position column. [Our guide to the generative AI report](/ai-search/search-console-generative-ai-report/) explains what it can and cannot show.

## Five actions for a business site

1. **Check the Search generative AI setting.** In Search Console, make sure your site is included. Google says this is needed to show in AI features and to qualify as a preferred source.
2. **Be worth the link.** Google's guide says content people find unique, compelling and useful will likely influence your presence in AI search more than any other suggestion in it. A direct answer near the top, then the evidence, is simply clear writing.
3. **Use real images.** The guide says supporting text with high-quality images can open more ways to appear, and related links now carry thumbnails. We have no evidence about where those thumbnails come from, so this is a reasonable precaution, not a tested tactic.
4. **If you publish news or regular updates, check the source tool.** Search for your domain at google.com/preferences/source. If it appears, Google's documentation offers a button and a plain link readers can use to add you.
5. **Record a baseline.** Note 15 October in your reports and save your Performance and generative AI figures from before and after. Do not add the two reports together, because the AI data is already inside the main one.

## Does it apply in Malaysia and Singapore?

Partly unknown. For Preferred Sources, Google's documentation says the feature is available globally in all languages where Search is available, and in AI features wherever those features run. For the carousels, Google says "additional locations and languages" and names none. For the other three changes it gives no country detail at all. We did not test whether the new layouts appear for Malaysian searches, so we cannot say what a reader in Kuala Lumpur sees today.

## What we still do not know

We do not know how often the official pages section will appear, or whether the tests will ship. We do not know how many extra clicks any change brings, and Google has not offered a figure. We do not know how Google decides which sites show up in the source preferences tool, or whether Search Console will ever report inline link clicks separately. We will update this piece as better evidence appears.
