AI Overviews used to be mostly a feature of question-style searches. New data suggests they now sit on most of the searches that come just before a purchase. In a study published on 21 October 2026, Semrush found an AI Overview on about 88% of 400 tracked commercial keywords in August 2026, up from about 46% in September 2025.

This piece reads that study with its limits in view, adds what Pew Research found about clicks, and says plainly what we cannot tell you about Malaysia.

## What the Semrush data shows

Semrush tracked a fixed panel of 400 commercial keywords across banking, ecommerce and software, grouped by five modifiers such as reviews, alternatives and "best X for Y". It checked each month from September 2025 to August 2026 whether an AI Overview appeared.

- **Overall:** about 88% of keywords in August 2026, from about 46% a year earlier.
- **Reviews:** from about 30% to nearly 90%, the biggest gain of any modifier group.
- **Ecommerce:** from about 33% to about 85%. Ecommerce price and cost keywords went from about 5% to about 71%.
- **By August 2026**, all five modifier groups were between 82% and 96%.

## What the data cannot tell you

- **It is a small, fixed panel.** Semrush says the results reflect those 400 keywords, not all commercial search.
- **It is US and desktop only.** It says nothing about Malaysian or mobile searches.
- **It measures presence, not impact.** Semrush says coverage does not show how AI Overviews affect clicks or traffic.

One detail cuts the other way. Semrush says the keywords without an AI Overview carried less than 3% of search volume, so weighted by volume the coverage was about 97%. In other words, almost all of the busiest commercial searches in the panel were covered.

On clicks, the nearest evidence is Pew Research. Among 900 US adults in March 2025, people clicked a normal result link in 8% of visits with an AI summary and 15% without, and clicked a link inside the summary in 1%. That covers all searches, not buying searches, and it is older than the Semrush data. Treat the two as a direction, not a forecast.

## Does it apply in Malaysia?

We do not know. None of the sources we found measured Malaysian commercial searches, and we have not run our own test, so we will not guess a number. What we can say is that Google's rollout of AI features is not limited to the US, as we covered in [what Google's new search means for Malaysian sites](/ai-search/google-ai-search-redesign-malaysia/). Our reading is that shops here competing on reviews and comparisons should expect similar pressure. That is an inference from US data, and results here may differ by language, category and device. The sensible step is to measure your own searches rather than assume.

## What a shop can do now

Google's guide to generative AI features says this is still SEO. Our reading of it for a shop:

1. **Search your own money terms from Malaysia.** Note the date, whether an AI Overview appears and which sites it cites. Repeat monthly.
2. **Keep product data current** in Merchant Center, and your Business Profile if you have a physical shop.
3. **Publish first-hand reviews and comparisons.** Google asks for content that is hard to copy, not summaries anyone could produce.
4. **Check Search Console's generative AI report** for impressions, and compare it with visits in your analytics. [Our guide](/ai-search/search-console-generative-ai-report/) explains what it leaves out.
