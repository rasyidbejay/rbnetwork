A headline says ChatGPT now handles a fifth of Google's search volume. The sentence behind it is more careful than the headline, and the part that is missing is the method.

## What was actually said

Adam Fry, who leads ChatGPT's consumer product, told Alex Kantrowitz of Big Technology that the volume of search-like queries on ChatGPT is now roughly one fifth the volume of Google's queries. Business Insider quoted him, and Big Technology published the interview on 3 November 2026, after an OpenAI-hosted dinner in New York.

Two words carry the weight: "search-like" and "queries". Neither is defined in the coverage we read. Search Engine Land reports that OpenAI has not explained how it counts searches or calculates the comparison, and explainx.ai notes that Google has not confirmed or disputed it. The interview also reportedly gave 1.2 billion weekly users, up from 1 billion two months earlier.

The [Search Engine Land headline](https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-1-trillion-searches-500589) turned this into a figure of 1 trillion searches a year. We could not open that article. OpenAI's own wording was a ratio, so any total depends on which Google figure someone multiplies it by.

## Why the definition matters

A ratio of "20%" means something different under each count. Counting every prompt gives one number. Counting only prompts where ChatGPT looked something up gives a smaller one. Counting every web query it sends could give a larger one. The comparison also depends on how Google's queries are counted, and on the period. Without the method, 20% is a claim, not a measurement.

## Other estimates, other methods

Peec AI, a vendor, estimated in October 2025 that ChatGPT handled 4 to 12% of Google's search volume. It used two methods. One started from OpenAI's own usage figures of 2.5 billion prompts a day with about 24% of them searches, and gave roughly 4%. The other combined referral traffic with click-through assumptions. Peec also cited an Ahrefs figure of 0.6%, based only on outgoing traffic.

These are older, they come from a vendor, and they define a search differently. They do not prove OpenAI wrong. They show how far the answer moves with the definition.

## Users, prompts and searches are different things

The 1.2 billion weekly users figure counts people, not queries, and it does not say how many of their prompts involve a search. MediaPost reported that web search was enabled on 34.5% of ChatGPT queries in February 2026, down from 46% in late 2024. The part of the report we read does not name who measured it, so treat it with care. It still makes the point: a large share of prompts are not searches at all, so the choice of denominator changes the ratio.

## Questions to ask of any AI search statistic

- Does it count prompts, prompts that use the web, or each web query?
- Which period and which countries does it cover?
- Who measured it, and can anyone outside check it?

## What it means for a site owner

- **A global ratio is not your traffic.** None of the coverage we read breaks the figure down by country, so it tells you nothing about Malaysia.
- **Measure your own referrals.** Our guide to [tracking ChatGPT and AI search traffic in GA4](/ai-search/track-ai-search-traffic-ga4/) shows how.
- **Ads are a separate question.** ChatGPT now runs ads in Malaysia and Singapore, covered in [our ChatGPT Ads guide](/ai-search/chatgpt-ads-malaysia-singapore/).

## What would settle it

OpenAI publishing its definition, the period and the denominator. Independent panel data that counts the same thing for both products. A statement from Google. Until then, say "OpenAI says" and not "ChatGPT has reached".
