SEO, AEO and GEO are three names for overlapping work. SEO is about being found and clicked in search results. AEO is about being the direct answer. GEO is about being cited in answers that an AI writes. For Google Search, Google says all three rest on the same foundation, which is pages that are indexed, original and useful. What changes is what you measure.

This explainer defines each term in one sentence, sets out what Google says, matches the terms to four kinds of business and gives you questions to ask an agency. For the evidence on whether GEO is truly separate, read [GEO vs SEO in 2026](/ai-search/geo-vs-seo-2026/). We do not repeat that analysis here.

## The short answer

- **SEO**, search engine optimisation: helping pages get found, ranked and clicked in search results.
- **AEO**, answer engine optimisation: helping a page become the direct answer to a question, in a featured snippet, a voice reply or an AI answer.
- **GEO**, generative engine optimisation: helping content get cited, and described accurately, inside answers that an AI writes.

## SEO: be found, ranked and clicked

SEO is the oldest of the three and the base for the other two. It covers whether Google can crawl and index your pages, whether the pages deserve to rank for the questions people ask, and whether the result earns the click. Its scoreboard is rankings, clicks and click-through rate, which you can read in Search Console's Performance report.

## AEO: be the direct answer

AEO aims at the moment when a search ends without a visit, because the answer is shown on the results page or spoken aloud. In practice that means a short, plain answer near the top of a page, supported by facts a reader can check. Google's guide lists AEO as a term people use for work on AI search visibility. It does not describe a separate Google programme for it.

## GEO: be cited and described accurately

GEO applies to answers that an AI writes by pulling from several sources. The goal is twofold: that your page is cited, and that the answer describes your business correctly. The term comes from a 2023 research paper by Pranjal Aggarwal and colleagues, published at the KDD conference in 2024, which tested content changes on its own benchmark. It was not a test on Google's live features, so treat it as the origin of the word, not a forecast for your site.

## What Google says

Google's guide to generative AI features, last updated on 10 July 2026, says "AEO" and "GEO" are terms people use for work on AI search visibility, and that optimising for generative AI search is optimising for the search experience, "and thus still SEO". It advises checking third-party AEO and GEO advice against its own guidance. It also says you do not need new machine-readable files, AI text files or special markup to appear in Google Search, and that structured data is not required for generative AI search.

That is a statement about Google Search. Other AI services run their own systems, and Google's guide does not speak for them.

## Which one do you need?

Our reading is that you do not pick one. You start with the shared foundation and then decide what to measure. The right emphasis depends on how people find and choose your business. This is our editorial judgement, not Google guidance.

**A local shop.** People ask where, when and how much. The most useful work is also the plainest: accurate name, hours, address and prices on your own site, and the same details wherever you control a listing. An AI answer that describes you wrongly can do more harm than one that leaves you out, so ask the tools you care about about your business now and then and note what they say.

**An online shop.** Product pages with real detail, such as sizes, materials, delivery and returns, serve shoppers and give any summary something solid to quote. Here the sale is the scoreboard. If you cannot tell which visits turned into orders, fix that before spending on anything labelled GEO.

**A B2B service.** Buyers ask comparison and how-to questions long before they enquire. Pages that answer them, with case evidence only you can supply, are the strongest asset. Your own experience cannot be copied from other pages, which is what Google's guide asks for.

**A publisher.** Original reporting is the point. If you publish news or regular updates, check whether your site appears in Google's Preferred Sources tool, as we describe in [our piece on Google's October link changes](/ai-search/google-ai-answers-more-links/). Citations and Search Console data both matter.

Whichever applies to you, choose one number to watch before you change anything: calls, orders, enquiries or subscribers. Then compare it before and after each piece of work, and keep clicks and AI impressions as supporting figures, not the goal. A change you cannot measure is a change you cannot judge, and an agency that will not agree a measure up front is telling you something.

If you also run paid ads on an AI service, that is a different decision. We cover it in [ChatGPT Ads in Malaysia and Singapore](/ai-search/chatgpt-ads-malaysia-singapore/).

## Questions to ask an agency

Whatever the agency calls its service, the questions are the same. Google's hiring page lists several of them, and we have added three.

1. **Can you show examples of previous work and success stories?** Ask for references and call at least one.
2. **Do you follow Google Search Essentials?** If the answer is vague, move on.
3. **What results do you expect, in what timeframe, and how will you measure them?** Google's page gives no timeframe of its own, so the agency has to commit to one.
4. **Will you share every change you make, and the reasoning?** You should be able to explain the work to someone else.
5. **What are you asking about my business?** A good provider asks about your customers, competitors and what makes you different.
6. **What would you do for GEO that you would not do for SEO?** Our question. A good answer names concrete tasks, not a new label.
7. **How do you report citations, and where does the data come from?** Our question. Google's generative AI report shows impressions only, as we explain in [our guide to the report](/ai-search/search-console-generative-ai-report/).
8. **What will you stop doing if it does not work?** Our question. It tests honesty.

### Red flags

Google's hiring page names several warning signs:

- A guarantee of first place. Google says no one can guarantee a number one ranking, and to find someone else if an SEO does.
- A claimed special relationship with Google, or a "priority submit".
- Secrecy, or refusing to explain what they plan to do.
- Tools said to be approved by Google. Google says it does not evaluate or endorse third-party SEO tools.
- Out-of-the-blue sales messages.
- Any request that you link to the SEO's own site.
- Write access to Search Console during an audit. Google says read access is enough at that stage.

Our reading is that "we can guarantee AI citations" belongs in the same list.

## A short glossary

- **AI Overviews**: a generative AI feature in Google Search that drafts an answer using content from the Search index.
- **AI Mode**: a second generative AI feature in Google Search that also draws on content from the Search index.
- **Grounding**: a technique, also called retrieval-augmented generation, in which an AI response relies on Search's ranking systems to retrieve relevant pages from the index.
- **Query fan-out**: a set of concurrent, related queries that the model generates to request more information. Google's example is a weedy lawn question that produces related queries about herbicides and weed prevention.
- **Citations**: the links or named sources an AI answer shows for its claims.
- **llms.txt**: a text file some site owners create for AI systems. Google says Google Search ignores such files, so creating one neither helps nor harms visibility there.
- **Structured data**: markup that labels what a page contains. Google says it is not required for generative AI search, but it remains useful for rich results.

## What we still do not know

We do not know how Google picks which eligible page to cite beyond the general description of grounding and query fan-out. We do not know whether other AI services will converge on the same signals as Google. And we have not seen a study of how Malay-language pages are cited compared with English ones. We will update this piece as better evidence appears.
