On 19 May 2026 Google announced a redesigned, AI-powered search box and a new default model for AI Mode. Five months on, the useful question for a Malaysian site owner is narrower than the headlines: which of those changes reach your visitors, and what should you actually do about them?

This piece separates what Google has confirmed from what it has not. Where we are reading between the lines, we say so.

## What Google changed in May

Google's own announcement lists four changes that affect ordinary searches:

- **A redesigned search box.** It accepts text, images, files, video and Chrome tabs, and began rolling out on 19 May in every country and language where AI Mode is available.
- **A new default model.** Gemini 3.5 Flash now powers AI Mode for all users worldwide.
- **Follow-up questions from AI Overviews.** A reader can continue from an AI Overview into AI Mode, with the context carried over. Google says this is live on desktop and mobile worldwide.
- **Agent features.** Google said background "information agents" would start with Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers "this summer". It described booking for local services as a United States rollout, and announced agents that call businesses in the same part of its post. We have not confirmed whether these have launched.

The first three change how people ask questions. The last one is aimed at subscribers and US users, and we found no Malaysian dates for it.

## Does it apply in Malaysia?

Google's post does not mention Malaysia or Malay. What we can piece together from press coverage is this: AI Mode reached some users in Malaysia in August 2025, in English only, and Malay replies followed after Google's October 2025 expansion. Later reporting on Google's February 2026 expansion also lists Malay, so the exact date Malay arrived is not settled in the sources we found.

Because Google ties the new search box to places where AI Mode is available, Malaysian searchers are very likely covered. That is our reading, not a statement from Google about Malaysia. For the agent features, there are no Malaysian dates at all.

## What Google tells site owners to do

Google's guide to generative AI features, last updated on 10 July 2026, is blunt: optimising for these features is still SEO. The main points:

- **Publish content that is hard to copy.** Original, first-hand or expert material beats summaries that anyone, or any model, could produce.
- **Keep the technical basics right.** Pages must be indexable and eligible to appear with a snippet, and the site must be included in generative AI features in Search Console.
- **Keep business data current.** Local businesses should use a Google Business Profile, and online shops should use Merchant Center.
- **Skip the extras.** Google says Search does not use llms.txt files, that you do not need to split content into chunks or rewrite it for AI systems, and that no special markup is required. Structured data is still worth using for rich results.
- **Do not manufacture mentions.** Seeking fake brand mentions across the web does not help, and mass-producing pages for every query variation falls under Google's scaled content abuse policy.

## New controls and a new report

On 3 June Google announced a Search Console control and a performance report for generative AI features. On 31 August it confirmed both are available to all sites worldwide.

The report shows impressions for your pages in these features, which pages appeared, the countries where they appeared, and trends over time. Device data is available for Search only. It shows no clicks and no search queries, and a site without enough data may not see the report yet.

The control lets a site opt out of AI Overviews, AI Mode and AI Overviews in Discover, both as links and as material used to ground answers. Google says an opted-out site gets no traffic or impressions from those features, and that the setting does not affect rankings in regular Search.

## Five checks for your site

1. **Confirm your pages can show a snippet.** Look for a nosnippet directive or a very low max-snippet value in your templates and plugins. Either can keep a page out of these features or limit how it appears.
2. **Open the generative AI report in Search Console.** Note which pages appear and whether Malaysia is among the countries. Because the report has no clicks, compare it with referral traffic in your own analytics.
3. **Decide on the opt-out on purpose.** The default is to be included. If you choose to opt out, you give up impressions and traffic from those features, so make it a business decision rather than a reaction.
4. **Fill in your business profiles.** If you serve local customers or sell products, complete your Google Business Profile or Merchant Center feed.
5. **Spend effort on the content, not the tricks.** An hour spent adding an original example, a local price or your own data is a better use of time than an llms.txt file, which Google says Search does not use.

## What we still do not know

Google has published no Malaysia-specific figures. Without clicks in the report, nobody outside Google can say how many Malaysian impressions turn into visits. The timing of the agent features outside the United States is also open. We will update this piece when Google says more.
